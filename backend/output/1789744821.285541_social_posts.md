### LinkedIn Post Summary

As generative AI tools like ChatGPT and instant search summaries become the default for students and professionals alike, we are living through a massive, real-time experiment in how instant answers affect the human mind. While AI unlocks unprecedented productivity, it comes with a hidden cognitive tax: the erosion of deep reading and sustained attention. When AI does the heavy lifting of boiling down complex texts, our brains miss out on the vital cognitive friction required for genuine comprehension.

This shift has introduced the "Illusion of Competence," where polished, authoritative AI outputs trick users into feeling they’ve mastered a subject when they've only experienced surface-level familiarity. Furthermore, the slot-machine-like feedback loop of AI prompting triggers dopamine spikes that make traditional, slow-paced tasks—like reading a book or tackling a blank page—feel excruciatingly difficult. In the workplace and classroom, this is creating a stark divide between "augmented thinkers" who use AI to amplify their expertise and the "AI-dependent" who risk severe skill degradation by bypassing critical thinking entirely.

Fortunately, a counter-movement is rising. The future of elite performance won't belong to the person who uses the most AI prompts, but to those who embrace "Slow Tech"—periodically stepping back to protect their focus, embrace cognitive friction, and preserve their attention span as an un-automatable asset. AI is an incredible co-pilot, but we must never let it take the wheel of our own minds.
